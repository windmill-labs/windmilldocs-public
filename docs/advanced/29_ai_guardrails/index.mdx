# Let business teams build with AI safely

Windmill lets business teams build scripts, flows and apps with AI, on the same platform and in the same workspaces as the engineers who run production.
Engineers keep control of what ships: the AI builds in an isolated copy, a reviewer deploys, and the code runs on workers you chose for it.

This page combines the guardrails Windmill already has into one setup for AI-assisted building, often called vibe coding.
Each guardrail is documented on its own page, linked below.
For an overview, see the [build with AI](/platform/build-with-ai) page.
For the general progression from a shared workspace to multi-environment promotion, see [collaboration and deployment stages](../23_canonical_deployment_setups/index.mdx).

| Guardrail | What it stops | Edition |
|---|---|---|
| [Workspace roles](#choose-what-each-team-can-author) | Authoring more than a team needs | Overriding the default roles on every edition, custom roles on [Enterprise Edition](/pricing) |
| [Folders and scoped AI context](#scope-what-the-ai-can-reach) | The AI reaching resources, skills and MCP servers a team should not use | All editions |
| [Forks and review](#build-in-a-fork-deploy-after-review) | Unreviewed changes reaching the shared workspace | Forks on all self-hosted editions, protection rulesets on Enterprise Edition |
| [Dev and prod workspaces](#promote-between-environments) | Builders holding prod credentials or editing prod | Dev workspaces on all self-hosted editions, locking prod on Enterprise Edition |
| [Dedicated workers](#run-their-jobs-on-separate-workers) | Unreviewed code running next to production jobs | Tags on all editions, worker group features on Enterprise Edition |
| [A separate instance](#keep-builders-on-a-separate-instance) | Any shared control plane with production | Enterprise Edition |
| [Audit logs](#audit-what-was-built-and-deployed) | Changes nobody can trace | Full audit logs on Enterprise Edition |

You don't need all of them.
Most teams start with roles, forks and review, then add dedicated workers or a separate instance when the code built by business teams touches sensitive systems.

## Choose what each team can author

A [workspace role](../../core_concepts/16_roles_and_permissions/index.mdx#workspace-roles) is a set of capabilities, and two of them suit business teams:

- Members who assemble what engineers built get **Compose** for flows and apps, without **Write code**. They build flows only from deployed workspace scripts and flows, with no inline code and no Hub scripts, and their full-code apps call existing workspace runnables and always deploy with [sandbox isolation](../../full_code_apps/8_sandbox_isolation/index.mdx).
- Members who write code with AI get **Write code**, and rely on the guardrails below instead of on the role. [AI sessions](../../core_concepts/69_ai_sessions/index.mdx) require Write code: members without it keep the AI chat side panel.

A role restricts authoring, not access to data: what a member can read and use is set by [folders and groups](#scope-what-the-ai-can-reach).
A role that can author, at the Compose or Full level, consumes a full seat.

## Scope what the AI can reach

The AI acts with the permissions of the person using it, so scoping a team scopes its AI.
Give each team a [folder](../../core_concepts/8_groups_and_folders/index.mdx) and grant its group access only to the [resources](../../core_concepts/3_resources_and_types/index.mdx) and [variables](../../core_concepts/2_variables_and_secrets/index.mdx) it needs.

The AI's context follows the same model:

- [Skills](../../core_concepts/69_ai_sessions/index.mdx#skills) and [MCP connections](../../core_concepts/69_ai_sessions/index.mdx#mcp-connections) saved in a folder are available to everyone who can read that folder, and nobody else.
- Workspace [instructions](../../core_concepts/69_ai_sessions/index.mdx#instructions), editable only by admins, are sent with every session in the workspace, for example to point the AI at the team's approved scripts and conventions.

A member with read access to a variable can read its value, secrets included, through the API, whatever their role.
Keep prod credentials out of the folders business teams can read, and give them their own values in a [dev workspace](#promote-between-environments).

## Build in a fork, deploy after review

An AI session runs in a workspace or in a [workspace fork](../20_workspace_forks/index.mdx), an isolated copy of the workspace.
Creating a fork per session keeps every experiment out of the shared workspace until someone reviews it.

To make review mandatory, add a [protection ruleset](../../core_concepts/56_protection_rulesets/index.mdx) that enables **Disable direct deployment** on the shared workspace, with a group of engineers on its bypass list.
Changes made in a fork then reach the shared workspace only when one of them reviews the diff on the [Compare & Deploy](../20_workspace_forks/index.mdx#merge-workspaces-from-the-ui-merge-ui) page and deploys it.
[CI tests](../24_ci_tests/index.mdx) run automatically when the tested script or flow is deployed.

A fork is a full copy of its parent, variables and secrets included.
If business teams should not hold prod credentials, have them build in a dev workspace instead.

## Promote between environments

A [dev workspace](../26_dev_workspaces/index.mdx) is a persistent fork paired with a prod workspace, with its own resource and variable values.
Business teams build in dev, against dev databases and accounts, and prod can be [locked](../26_dev_workspaces/index.mdx#locking-the-prod-workspace) so changes only reach it through promotion.

Promotion is never automatic: a member who can deploy to prod reviews the diff and deploys it from [Compare & Deploy](../26_dev_workspaces/index.mdx#promoting-changes-to-prod), or the change goes through a pull request with [Promote to prod via Git](../26_dev_workspaces/index.mdx#promote-to-prod-via-git).
Resources and variables marked [workspace-specific](../../core_concepts/12_staging_prod/index.md#workspace-specific-resources-and-variables) keep their value in each environment, so promoting code never overwrites a prod credential.
When deploying, [run on behalf of](../../core_concepts/12_staging_prod/index.md#run-on-behalf-of) decides which user the item executes as in prod, for example a service account rather than the person who built it.

## Run their jobs on separate workers

[Worker groups](../../core_concepts/9_worker_groups/index.mdx) decide where code runs, and can sit on different machines, clusters and networks.
Route the jobs of business teams to their own workers:

- [Restrict a tag to specific workspaces](../../core_concepts/9_worker_groups/index.mdx#restrict-tags-to-specific-workspaces), so only the dev workspace can use the workers that reach dev systems, and prod-only workers stay out of reach. A fork inherits a scoped tag only when you opt in with `*`.
- Use [`$workspace` in a tag](../../core_concepts/9_worker_groups/index.mdx#dynamic-tag), so the same script runs on `normal-dev` workers in the dev workspace and on `normal-prod` workers once promoted. A dev workspace resolves `$workspace` to itself, so it can have its own workers.
- Send all fork jobs to a [dedicated fork worker pool](../20_workspace_forks/index.mdx#dedicated-fork-workers-optional) with the `-fork` tag suffix.

For code that has not been reviewed yet, add [process isolation](../27_security_isolation/index.mdx) on those workers, or run them as [agent workers](../../core_concepts/28_agent_workers/index.mdx) without direct database access.

## Keep builders on a separate instance

For the strictest separation, business teams build on their own Windmill instance, with its own database, workers and settings, and engineers promote reviewed changes to the production instance.
[Deploying to a workspace on another instance](../../core_concepts/12_staging_prod/index.md#deploy-to-a-workspace-on-another-instance) from the deploy drawer runs as the deployer's own account on the prod instance, so its permissions, protection rules and audit logs apply.
The [Git promotion workflow](../9_deploy_gh_gl/index.mdx) works across instances too.

## Audit what was built and deployed

Every action with side effects is recorded in the [audit logs](../../core_concepts/14_audit_logs/index.mdx) with the user who performed it, including deployments.
Each script, flow and app keeps its [version history](../../core_concepts/34_versioning/index.mdx), so a promoted change can be compared or rolled back.
On self-hosted Enterprise Edition, audit logs are kept for 365 days by default and can be forwarded to a SIEM.

## Example setup

A team of analysts building internal tools with AI, next to a platform team that runs production:

1. Workspace `ops` is prod. A protection ruleset disables direct deployment, with the platform team on the bypass list.
2. Dev workspace `ops-dev` is paired with it, with dev database credentials marked workspace-specific.
3. Analysts have a role with Write code in `ops-dev`, and an operator role in `ops`.
4. Scripts are tagged `normal-$workspace`: jobs run on `normal-ops-dev` workers in a network that only reaches dev systems, and on `normal-ops` workers once promoted.
5. Analysts build in AI sessions, one fork of `ops-dev` per session, and deploy into `ops-dev` when it works.
6. The platform team reviews the diff from `ops-dev` to `ops` in Compare & Deploy and deploys it.
